Research

From insurance portfolios to individual behaviour.

My work develops statistically principled methods that remain useful in practice—combining probability, Bayesian modelling, latent-state methods, machine learning, and granular insurance data.

01

Claim reserving & pricing

Micro-level and aggregate reserving, reporting delays, IBNR estimation, population-sampling foundations, and coherent links between reserves and premiums.

02

Telematics & behavioural risk

High-frequency driving signals, hidden Markov models, anomaly detection, temporal motifs, collision detection, and dynamic risk indices.

03

Insurance analytics & ratemaking

Mixture-of-experts models, risk classification, random effects, censored and truncated loss data, and interpretable predictive modelling.

04

Mathematical risk theory

Dependence, ruin theory, Markov-additive processes, stochastic fluid flows, matrix-analytic methods, and operational risk.

“Actuarial models should be mathematically coherent, statistically defensible, and useful for decisions.”

I joined the University of Toronto in 2006, following doctoral studies in actuarial science at Western University and postdoctoral work at the University of Waterloo. I became a full professor in 2020.

My earlier work focused on mathematical risk theory, queueing theory, stochastic fluid flows, and matrix-analytic methods. That foundation continues to shape my current research in claim reserving, ratemaking, insurance data science, and telematics.

I served as Director of the Master of Financial Insurance program from 2022 to 2025 and as Interim Chair of the Department of Statistical Sciences in 2024. I am an Associate Editor of Insurance: Mathematics and Economics and the Annals of Actuarial Science.
